How to Tell Fatigue From Extraction in a Failing Institution

Briar Morgan Greenway · ThinkWell Labs

Some institutional failure is fatigue: budgets thin out, people burn out, systems age past what they were built for. Some of it is extraction: the value is drained on purpose while the shell keeps its name. From the outside the two look the same. Service gets worse, trust falls, and everyone argues about whose fault it is without any way to settle it.

I built Toroid to settle it with measurement instead of volume.

What gets attacked

Toroid starts from one idea: every society runs on a load-bearing layer, the people and functions it can't do without. Extraction goes after that layer. Fatigue wears it down; extraction cuts beneath it and takes what it carried. The difference is structural, so it can be counted.

Nine forms, one ring

Toroid is a system of nine forms for seeing that one thing. Each form is a single measurement; some you operate, some you read. They run in the order the work is done: observe, name, measure, predict, check, correct.

  • Polymorphic Catalysis observes the actors, the targets and the back-channels between them.
  • Polymorphic Matrix is the precursor and archive, the first map of the circulation.
  • The Substrate Attack names the live condition: the cut beneath every loop.
  • The Removal Economy observes and measures extraction as one loop with six stations.
  • Obfuscratic Sabbotarchy measures with an engineered-incompetence index.
  • Sches is the generator as play, a strategy game where the same dynamics run on a board.
  • The Two Cycles shows how the forms fit together and where the gaps are.
  • The System holds the papers, the terminology, the method and the legal terms.
  • Named and Counted is the companion volume, in full.

Evidence first, verdict last

Everything in Toroid is documented from public sources and measured. Interpretation is offered as interpretation, not as allegation. That distinction matters to me: an instrument that can only convict is a weapon, not a measurement.

The book

Named and Counted, Not Blamed nor Accounted is the long form. It scores seven federal architectures against a control group on five signatures and reports a 0 to 100 index. It reads the shape of a failure, not its size, so you can argue about accountability with numbers instead of rhetoric. It can acquit as well as convict.

Start with the forms at toroid.fyi. If you want the whole argument, read the book.
