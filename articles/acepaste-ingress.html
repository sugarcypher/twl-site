Prompt Injection From Web Pages: A Gate Before Your Agent

Briar Morgan Greenway · ThinkWell Labs

If your AI agent reads the web, any page can talk to it. The page you see and the page your agent reads aren't always the same: text pushed off screen, colored to match the background, tucked into attributes and comments, or written in invisible characters. To you it's a kettle review. To the model it's a set of instructions.

AcePaste Ingress stands at the door between the web and your AI. Every page is checked before the model reads it.

Three verdicts

  • Clean. The model gets the visible text inside a provenance envelope, with a signed receipt.
  • Suspect. Nothing is delivered. A person can override it, and the receipt records that they did.
  • Hostile. Nothing is delivered, and it can't be overridden. The refusal is logged.

Ingress refuses by default. Anything the model would receive that a person viewing the page wouldn't see is suspect. Internal errors count as refusals, not passes.

What it checks

  • Invisible Unicode: tag characters, zero-width channels, bidirectional overrides, look-alike letters.
  • Hidden text: off screen, hidden by CSS, or colored to match its background.
  • Payloads in attributes, comments, SVG and srcdoc.
  • Cloaking: it fetches each page twice and compares, and can render it in a throwaway browser to compare again.

On the record

Every verdict gets a receipt, clean or refused, hash-chained and signed, and you can verify them offline. Every refusal is logged with where the finding was, what the hidden payload said once decoded, and which hosts were contacted. Each finding is labeled as an inference or hypothesis, with a confidence band and a possible harmless explanation.

After your agent reads outside content, side-effecting tool calls need your approval, one call at a time, through a channel the model can't control. Secrets and keys are redacted from what's delivered.

Install

It works with MCP agents such as Claude Desktop, Claude Code and Cursor, which get ingress_fetch and ingress_scan tools. To try it from a terminal: npx @acepaste/ingress https://example.com. It runs on your own machine or server; no ThinkWell Labs service sits in the loop, and your logs and receipts stay with you.

Measured, honestly

On version 0.9.1, against its own test corpus: 90 of 90 attacks refused, 48 of 48 ordinary documents passed clean, 3 of 3 ambiguous documents held as suspect, never hostile, with a median analysis time of 0.3 milliseconds. Those figures set a floor. They are not a claim about the open web.

What it doesn't do

Its judgment of visible prose is deliberately narrow: only the strongest instruction-like text is refused, and those refusals can be overridden. The approval broker governs only the calls routed through it. It's pre-1.0, so interfaces may still change.

It's free on npm and source-available.
